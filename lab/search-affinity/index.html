Search Affinity Lab

Which search twins are real?

Two searches that move together over five years can be a coincidence. So we ran three separate tests on every pair. The more tests a pair passes, the more likely the same people are behind both searches.

Everything combined

Six things the data says when you mix it

We put every dataset from this project side by side: five years of search, state maps, the age and gender of searchers, company sales, government spending data and client surveys. These are the patterns that show up in more than one source.

1. The med spa map is the Pilates map

0.81 match

We combined eight treatment searches into one score per state. The state ranking that matches it best is Pilates (0.81), then Tesla, Rolex, Love Island and Uber Eats (0.74 to 0.75). The opposite map belongs to fishing licenses and crockpots (−0.66).

Top states: Florida, Arizona, Colorado, Utah and California. Bottom: Vermont, South Dakota, Iowa and West Virginia. Much of this is the map of city life and money, so treat it as a wallet signal, not proof that one causes the other.

5. Brand search moves first

For any local business, the number to watch is searches for your own name next to searches for your category. When the first falls and the second doesn't, a competitor is taking share. See the charts.

The method

Three tests, not one

Each beauty search was checked against 40 everyday searches (food, drinks, apps, shows, hobbies). A match on one test is a fun chart. A match on all three is an audience.

1 · Time

Do they rise and fall together?

Five years of monthly Google search interest. The pair must line up month to month (0.50 or higher) and in its seasonal ups and downs once the long-term trend is removed (0.40 or higher). It also has to match better than the beauty search does with dull controls like “mattress” and “Costco.”

2 · Place

Are they big in the same states?

Google search interest across 49 states (Wyoming and DC left out), ranked and compared. The pair passes at a rank match of 0.35 or higher with less than a 1% chance of being luck.

3 · People

Is it the same crowd?

Age and gender of the people searching, from DataForSEO's search panel. The pair passes if the age mix of the two searches overlaps by at least 85% and the female share is within 10 points.

Why it matters

How a brand would use this

When to spend

A time match says when interest in both rises. Run the ad or the offer in the months where both lines climb, not across the whole year.

Where to spend

A place match says which states care about both. Put more budget in those states and less in the states where both are low.

Who to partner with

A place and people match points to a shared customer. Botox and Pilates are an example: the same states, the same age mix and nearly the same share of women search both, so a med spa and a Pilates studio running a joint offer would be talking to people who already want both.

Search vs. sales

Does search predict real sales?

We matched quarterly Google search interest against what public companies actually reported in sales, from early 2023 to Q3 2026. Both lines on each chart start at 100, so you can compare their direction even though one is searches and the other is dollars.

Jeuveau: brand search tracks brand sales

10 of 11 quarters

When searches for “jeuveau” grew year over year, Evolus revenue grew too, in 10 of 11 quarters. Over 15 quarters the two levels line up at 0.80.

Search for “jeuveau”Evolus revenue

The one miss is Q3 2026: search fell 5% while revenue rose 13%, helped by Evolus's newer filler line. The sizes of the changes don't line up well (0.80 is mostly the shared climb), so search tells you the direction, not the amount.

Botox: “botox” is a category word

Search up, sales down

In 2025 searches for “botox” rose 10% to 16% year over year while AbbVie's Botox Cosmetic sales fell 5% to 12%. Over 15 quarters the two barely relate (0.13).

Search for “botox”Botox Cosmetic sales (global)

People type “botox” for any wrinkle shot, the way they say Kleenex for any tissue. The same year, Jeuveau searches and sales grew. So “botox” measures demand for the whole category, not for the Botox brand. One more gap: AbbVie reports global sales and we measured U.S. search.

Club Pilates: brand search fell before sales did

Down 17%

Searches for “club pilates” were down 17% year over year in Q3 2026. Xponential, which owns Club Pilates, reported same-store sales down 7.5% across its brands and 5.6% at Club Pilates that quarter.

Search for “pilates”Search for “club pilates”

Growth in brand searches slowed from 15% in Q1 2025 to about zero by mid-2025, then turned negative in 2026. Xponential's North America same-store sales turned negative in Q3 2025 (down 1%) and kept falling, to down 4.3% in Q4 2025 and 7.5% by Q3 2026. Meanwhile searches for plain “pilates” were still up 17% in Q2 2026. The category kept growing, the brand did not.

The rule

Brand-name search tracks that brand's sales direction. A generic word like “botox” or “pilates” tracks the category.

For a local business

Watch searches for your own name. If they drop while the category holds, you are losing share, often a quarter or two before it shows in the books.

The limit

Three companies and 15 quarters is a small test. Search gets the direction right far more often than the size of the change.

Who to package with

Pilates and boutique fitness

Fitness shows up in the wallet data, and Botox and Pilates search in the same states with nearly the same age mix. A joint class-plus-treatment offer is the clearest fit.

Nails, blowouts and spas

The everyday beauty spend sits right above aesthetics on the cut list. These visits are frequent, so they are a steady place to reach the same client.

Weddings and big dates

Botox and “wedding dress” passed all three search tests, and microneedling lines up with “engagement ring.” Bridal shops and planners are a natural referral loop.

Beauty retail

In a Placer.ai case study, IV bar visitors also favored Sephora and Ulta. Retail beauty counters reach clients between treatments.

To make this exact for one city: Placer.ai's Favorite Places report on a client's own location lists the other businesses its visitors go to most. That is the true share-of-wallet answer, and the next thing to buy if we want to sell this as a product.

What this can and can't tell you

  • Passing all three tests still isn't proof that one search causes the other. It is strong evidence of a shared audience. Something else, like income, city life or summer, can drive both.
  • We tested 680 pairs. With that many tries, a handful of state matches (about 7 at our bar) will look real by chance. A pair that passes two or three separate tests is much less likely to be luck.
  • Race and ethnicity are not available. Google and DataForSEO report age and gender only. We did not guess race from state or city data.
  • Age and gender come from DataForSEO's search panel, not from Google. They are rough for small searches. We used the plain term (for example “botox,” not “botox near me”) where the “near me” version was too small.
  • Google changed how it counts searches in 2025 and 2026, which makes many terms rise together. That is why the time test also requires a seasonal match and beating the controls.
