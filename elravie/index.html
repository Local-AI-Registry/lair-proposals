Before you spend

Three things no other launch had to solve

Letybo and Skinvive launched with FDA approval and a clean story. Re2O has neither yet. These three sit in front of every dollar below.

The FDA question is open

L&C Bio says Re2O fits the human-tissue rules that let skin grafts sell without FDA approval. The FDA told Business Insider that powdered donor skin injected as a cosmetic booster may raise significant questions and may need a fuller approval process.

So what: settle this in writing before any consumer spend. A warning letter after launch costs more than the whole demand budget.

The story got out first

Business Insider (Oct 21) and NPR's Here & Now (Oct 30) told US readers it is made from dead Americans' skin. US searches for Re2O hit their high the same month: 1,300.

So what: many first searchers are curious or alarmed, not booking. The first answer they read, on Google or in ChatGPT, decides which.

It is already listed here

We found US clinics in Manhattan, LA Koreatown and Brea, CA with Re2O treatment pages, before the exclusive US partner has shipped. Where their product comes from is not public.

So what: verify every early listing. Turn the good ones into trained launch accounts. Ask the rest to take the page down.

    Where to put money

    One patient, every dollar, and what to fund more

    We traced every manufacturer dollar behind one new patient, then asked which dollars actually changed her mind.

    The most impactful manufacturer spend
    Training the injector. About $13 per patient, and it carries more of the decision than every ad combined.

    With a new human-tissue product, the patient asks the injector two things: is it safe, and will it work on me. A trained injector can answer both. Technique matters too: clinics that use it say depth and even spread decide the result, and bumps and nodules have been reported when they are off. The trial evidence is still one small study, so the injector's confidence carries the sale.

    "More large, randomized controlled clinical trials are needed."Y. Claire Chang, MD, New York dermatologist, in ELLE on human-tissue boosters

    How the math works

    Plays: Korean-booster pull below 80% of the US average is "Educate first", above 115% is "Seed now", otherwise "Build both". Reps go to cities by a demand score (45% skin-quality searches, 25% Rejuran searches, 15% Re2O searches, 15% practice count). Each play splits its city's demand budget differently.

    Reps needed equals the sales target divided by what one rep's book sells by the deadline. Influence is the drop in conversion if one step is removed; impact per dollar is influence divided by cost.
