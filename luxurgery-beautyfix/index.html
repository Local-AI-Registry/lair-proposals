How BeautyFix sells so many injectables

A look at what BeautyFix MedSpa in NYC is doing, with screenshots, and what it means for Luxurgery.

Subject: BeautyFix MedSpa, New York Captured: November 22, 2026 By: Local AI Registry
The short version

BeautyFix runs its practice like an online store. Treatments have product names and prices, they can be bought with a click, there is always a deal running, and prepaid memberships bring patients back every few months.

Part 1

The teardown

Six plays we found on their website, Google and Groupon.

Play 1

There is always a deal, with a clock running

  • Black Friday banner says "up to 70% off" across toxin, filler, skin, body and memberships.
  • Prices are right on the homepage: Botox from $229, Lip Flip $99, memberships up to 45% off.
  • A countdown timer sits at the top of every page. Savings apply in the cart, no code needed.
Why it works: a visitor sees a price and a deadline in the first five seconds. That removes the two biggest reasons people put off booking.
Play 2

Treatments are sold like products

  • Every service has a branded name: WrinkleFix, LipFix, CheekFix, FaceFix.
  • Each one has a price and a "Shop Now" button. The site is built on an e-commerce platform, so patients can pay before they ever walk in.
  • First-time Botox is priced lower ($199) than for existing customers ($495) to pull in new patients.
Why it works: a patient who has already paid almost always shows up. Branded names also make the menu easy to remember and easy to share.
Play 3

Prepaid memberships lock in a year of visits

  • Three annual plans: Unlimited Tox at $1,495, Elite at $2,495 and Ultimate at $4,995.
  • Tox members come in every three months. Higher plans add filler, Sculptra and microneedling.
  • Plans are prepaid for twelve months and the terms say they are non-refundable.
Why it works: the money comes in up front, and the patient is booked four times a year without any new marketing. Each visit is also a chance to add filler or skin treatments.
Weak spot: non-refundable, prepaid terms can cause complaints when a patient's plans change.
Play 4

Groupon brings in first-time patients

  • Lip flip, Botox and Juvederm deals at up to 50% off.
  • The listing shows 636+ Groupon reviews, which means years of steady use.
Why it works: Groupon puts them in front of bargain shoppers they would never reach on their own. Then the in-office team sells the membership.
Weak spot: it trains patients to expect a discount and puts the brand next to budget offers.
Play 5

They show up three times on Google

  • Three separate Google listings: the Flatiron med spa, the Upper East Side skin clinic and a plastic surgery listing.
  • Google's AI Overview describes all three by name, using what is on those listings.
Why it works: more listings means more space on the page and more chances to be the one AI names.
Weak spot: a competitor, Thérapie Clinic, is running a paid ad above BeautyFix on a search for BeautyFix. Even a big brand can lose patients on its own name.
Play 6

Big social proof and injectors people ask for by name

  • Their homepage claims 25,000+ patients treated and 1,000+ five-star reviews.
  • Reviews keep naming individual injectors. The top Google review in the screenshot above recommends one by name.
  • They also offer many ways to pay over time, including CareCredit, PatientFi and Affirm.
Why it works: patients trust a person more than a brand. Payment plans make a $1,500 membership feel like a monthly bill.
Part 2

What this means for Luxurgery

Luxurgery already has the clinical credibility BeautyFix is trying to buy with discounts. The gap is in how easy it is to say yes.

What Luxurgery has

  • A full injectables menu, with every major toxin and filler
  • Signature treatments with their own names, like Surgery in a Syringe℠
  • Dr. S as a known name, with real media and research
  • A booking button on the homepage

What the homepage doesn't show

  • No memberships or recurring plans
  • No prices or "starting at" ranges
  • No payment plan options
  • Booking sends patients off-site to Zocdoc

  1. Launch a prepaid injectables membership

    An annual plan built around Dr. S's practice, at full price instead of a discount. It turns one-time Botox visits into revenue you can count on every quarter, and it keeps patients from shopping around.

  2. Package the signature treatments so patients can book and pay online

    Surgery in a Syringe℠ is already a brand. Give it a clear page, a "starting at" price and a way to put down a deposit online, so a patient who is ready can commit right away.

  3. Bring booking onto Luxurgery's own website

    Right now the booking button leads to Zocdoc, where other doctors are one tap away. Booking on your own site keeps the patient and the data with you.

  4. Show payment plans

    Listing financing options next to treatments and memberships makes the decision feel smaller, especially for higher-ticket treatments.

  5. Turn the press and TV story into AI citations

    A story like "a famous NYC doctor uses AI to grow his practice" travels. Each article and segment becomes a source ChatGPT and Google cite when someone asks who the best injector in NYC is.

  6. Get named in AI answers for the searches that matter

    BeautyFix shows up in Google's AI Overview mostly because of its listings, not because of deliberate AI work. That leaves an opening for Luxurgery to be the name AI gives for high-value injectable and surgery questions in NYC.

  7. Protect searches for "Luxurgery" and "Dr. S"

    If a competitor can buy an ad above BeautyFix on its own name, it can happen to Luxurgery too. We can watch for it and make sure Luxurgery owns the top of those results.

What we would not copy

Groupon and "up to 70% off" sales. They work for a high-volume med spa, but they would pull the Luxurgery brand down. Memberships and online booking give you the same repeat business without the discounting.
