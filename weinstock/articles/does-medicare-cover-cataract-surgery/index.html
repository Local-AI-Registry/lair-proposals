What Medicare Covers for Cataract Surgery

Medicare covers medically necessary cataract surgery, but not every lens or upgrade. Here's what's included, what you may pay, and the questions to ask before surgery.

Key takeaways
  • Medicare Part B may cover cataract surgery that implants a conventional intraocular lens.
  • After the Part B deductible, you generally pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount; a Medigap policy can help with that share.
  • Premium lenses that also correct astigmatism or near vision cost extra, and Medicare must always let you choose a standard lens.
  • After each covered cataract surgery with a lens implant, Part B covers one pair of standard eyeglasses or one set of contacts.

What Medicare covers

Medicare Part B (Medical Insurance) may cover cataract surgery that implants a conventional intraocular lens.1 Coverage applies when your doctor determines surgery is medically necessary, typically because the cataract is affecting your daily life.2

Cataract surgery removes the cloudy natural lens and, in most cases, replaces it with a clear artificial lens.1

What you may pay

For Original Medicare, after you meet the Part B deductible, you generally pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount.2 If you have a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy, it can cover all or part of that 20% coinsurance.3

Costs vary by where surgery is performed. Medicare's online procedure price lookup can give you an estimate, and our team will walk you through your own numbers before surgery.

Premium lenses and upgrades

Medicare pays for what it takes to restore functional vision, which is surgery with a conventional lens. If you choose a lens that also corrects astigmatism or reduces your need for reading glasses, you can pay the difference for the premium portion.4

Compare premium lens implant options. Two protections are worth knowing. Medicare patients must always be able to choose the standard lens, and a surgeon cannot require a premium lens as a condition of surgery.4

“A premium lens is a choice, never a requirement. We show patients exactly what each option does and what it costs, in writing, before anything is scheduled.”

DR. ROBERT J. WEINSTOCK · CATARACT & REFRACTIVE SURGEON

If you have Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage plans must provide at least the same coverage as Original Medicare, which includes cataract surgery with a standard lens.5 Some plans add vision benefits. Advantage plans may require precertification before a practice can bill you for a premium lens upgrade, so we check with your plan in advance.6

Glasses after cataract surgery

Medicare usually does not cover eyeglasses or contact lenses. The exception: after each cataract surgery that implants an intraocular lens, Part B covers one pair of eyeglasses with standard frames or one set of contact lenses.2 They must come from a supplier enrolled in Medicare.5

Questions to ask before surgery

  • Is my surgery considered medically necessary, and is my lens choice covered?
  • What will I pay for the surgeon, the surgery center and any lens upgrade?
  • Does my Medicare Advantage plan require approval before surgery?
  • Which lens fits my eyes and lifestyle, and what does each option do differently?

Frequently asked questions

Does Medicare cover laser cataract surgery?

Medicare covers medically necessary cataract surgery. Some laser steps used for extra refractive benefits, such as correcting astigmatism, can carry an added cost. Ask for a written quote before surgery.

Does Medicare pay for both eyes?

Medicare covers cataract surgery for each eye when surgery is medically necessary for that eye. Most patients have the eyes done on separate days.

Will Medicare pay for my glasses after surgery?

After each cataract surgery that implants a lens, Part B covers one pair of eyeglasses with standard frames or one set of contacts from a Medicare-enrolled supplier.

Can I choose a premium lens with Medicare?

Yes. You can choose a premium lens and pay the difference for the premium portion, but you can always choose the standard lens that Medicare covers.
