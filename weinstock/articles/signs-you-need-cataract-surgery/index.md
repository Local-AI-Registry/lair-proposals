# 7 Signs It May Be Time for Cataract Surgery

Cataracts creep in slowly, so most people adapt without noticing. Here are the seven signs a cataract may be affecting your life, and how to know when surgery makes sense.

**Key takeaways**

  * A cataract is a clouding of the eye's natural lens, and it usually develops slowly.
  * Common signs include blurry or dim vision, glare and halos, poor night vision and faded colors.

  * Cataract surgery replaces the cloudy lens with a clear artificial lens, and the right lens can also reduce your need for glasses.

## What is a cataract?

A cataract is a clouding of the lens inside your eye, which is normally clear.1 The lens sits behind the colored part of your eye and focuses light, so as it clouds, the image you see turns blurry, dim and washed out.

Age is the biggest risk factor. By age 80, more than half of all Americans either have a cataract or have had cataract surgery.2 Diabetes, long-term steroid use, eye injuries, previous eye surgery and years of sun exposure can all speed things up.1

## The 7 signs to watch for

Because cataracts usually grow slowly, early changes are easy to miss.3 These are the signs eye surgeons hear about most often:

### 1\. Cloudy, blurry or dim vision

It can feel like looking through a smudged or foggy window, even with clean glasses.3

### 2\. Glare and halos around lights

Headlights, lamps or sunlight may seem too bright, or you may see rings around lights.2

### 3\. Trouble seeing at night

Night driving is often the first thing people start avoiding.2

### 4\. Colors that look faded or yellowed

Whites can look dingy and blues can look dull, a change many patients only notice after surgery.3

### 5\. Frequent prescription changes

If your glasses or contacts never seem quite right for long, a changing lens may be the reason.3

### 6\. Double vision in one eye

Seeing a ghost image with one eye closed can be a sign of a cataract.3

### 7\. Needing more light to read

If you keep turning up the lamp or reaching for a flashlight to read menus, the lens may be letting in less light.4

**See a doctor right away** if you notice a sudden shower of floaters, flashes of light or a curtain over part of your vision. Those are usually not cataract symptoms and can signal a retinal emergency.

## When is it time for surgery?

There is rarely a rush. In general, cataract surgery makes sense when blurry vision starts to get in the way of everyday life, like driving, reading, working or enjoying hobbies.1

A comprehensive eye exam is the only way to confirm a cataract and rule out other conditions that cause similar symptoms.1

## What cataract surgery involves

Cataract surgery removes the cloudy natural lens and, in most cases, replaces it with a clear artificial lens called an intraocular lens (IOL).5 It is an outpatient procedure.

Today's lens options can do more than restore clarity. With the right lens, cataract surgery can also correct nearsightedness, farsightedness or astigmatism, which means less dependence on glasses afterward. ## Frequently asked questions

Can cataracts go away on their own?

No. A cataract does not clear up on its own. Brighter lighting and updated glasses can help for a while, but surgery is the only way to remove a cataract.

Is there harm in waiting?

For most people, waiting is safe as long as vision is not interfering with daily life or safety, such as driving. Your eye surgeon can tell you whether waiting makes sense for your eyes.

Does cataract surgery hurt?

Cataract surgery is performed with numbing drops, and most patients describe feeling pressure rather than pain.

Can I get rid of my glasses after cataract surgery?

Many patients can reduce their need for glasses by choosing a lens that also corrects nearsightedness, farsightedness or astigmatism. Your consultation will cover which lenses fit your eyes and lifestyle.
