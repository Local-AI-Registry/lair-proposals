EVO ICL (Implantable Collamer Lens) is a thin lens placed inside the eye, behind the iris and in front of your natural lens, to correct nearsightedness with or without astigmatism. Unlike LASIK, it does not remove corneal tissue and can be removed if needed. It is often a strong option for higher prescriptions, thin corneas or dry eye concerns.

**Who it suits** Nearsighted adults, including high prescriptions

**Corneal tissue removed** None

**Reversible** The lens can be removed

**FDA approved** March 2022 in the U.S.

## How EVO ICL works

The lens is folded and placed through a tiny opening, then unfolds behind the iris. Because it adds focusing power rather than reshaping the cornea, it can treat prescriptions that are too high for laser correction. The FDA approved the EVO ICL in the United States in March 2022.

## Who is a good candidate

EVO ICL is often considered for people with moderate to high nearsightedness, corneas that are thin or irregular, or dry eye that makes corneal surgery less appealing. Measurements of the space inside your eye confirm whether the lens will fit.

## What to expect

  1. **Evaluation.** Precise measurements choose the lens size and power.
  2. **Procedure.** Numbing drops and a short outpatient procedure for each eye.
  3. **Recovery.** Many people see well within a day or two, with follow-up visits to check eye pressure and lens position.

## EVO ICL FAQ

Is EVO ICL better than LASIK?

Neither is better for everyone. EVO ICL is often a better fit for higher prescriptions, thin corneas or dry eye, while LASIK suits many people with moderate prescriptions and healthy corneas.

Can EVO ICL be removed?

Yes. The lens is placed without removing corneal tissue and can be removed or exchanged if needed.

Will I feel the lens?

No. The lens sits inside the eye behind the iris, where it cannot be felt or seen by others.

Does EVO ICL treat astigmatism?

A toric version of the lens corrects nearsightedness with astigmatism.

Will I still need reading glasses later?

EVO ICL corrects distance vision but does not stop presbyopia, so reading glasses may be needed after about age 45.
