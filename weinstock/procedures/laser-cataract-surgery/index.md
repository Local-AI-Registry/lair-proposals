## How laser cataract surgery works

A cataract is a clouding of the lens inside the eye. During surgery, the cloudy lens is removed and replaced with an intraocular lens (IOL). With laser-assisted surgery, a femtosecond laser makes the opening in the lens capsule and softens the cataract so it can be removed gently, with imaging that maps your eye before the laser begins.

## What to expect, step by step

  1. **Consultation and measurements.** Detailed scans of your eye help choose the lens power and type.
  2. **Choosing your lens.** A standard lens, or a premium lens that can also correct astigmatism or reduce the need for reading glasses.

## Laser Cataract Surgery FAQ

Is laser cataract surgery better than traditional cataract surgery?

Both are safe and effective. The laser adds precision to certain steps and can help correct astigmatism at the same time. Dr. Weinstock will recommend the approach that fits your eyes.

Does cataract surgery hurt?

The eye is numbed with drops, and most patients feel only light pressure. You are awake but relaxed, and the procedure is usually brief.

How soon will I see clearly?

Many patients notice clearer vision within a day or two, and vision keeps improving over the following weeks as the eye heals.

Can both eyes be done on the same day?

Most patients have each eye treated on a separate day, usually a week or two apart, so the first eye can heal.

Will I still need glasses?

That depends on the lens you choose. Premium lenses can reduce or remove the need for glasses for many activities.
