Questions about the category

Do the antioxidants boost the SPF?

No. Sun protection comes only from zinc oxide and is exactly what the Drug Facts label says. The skincare ingredients do a separate job.

How deep do the ingredients go?

Published research has shown that certain topical antioxidants can reach the living epidermal layers of the skin. That is where skincare does its work, and it is all the depth skincare needs.

Why pair mineral filters with antioxidants?

Published lab research (Bernstein et al., 2020) found that experimental formulas containing zinc oxide kept more of their overall antioxidant activity after UV exposure than formulas without UV filters. The white paper treats this as preliminary, ingredient-level research, not a test of Mama Sol's finished formulas. Read the white paper

Isn't this just sunscreen with antioxidants?

Most sunscreens are built to block, with skincare added later. Biological Suncare™ starts with both jobs from day one, and every formula has to meet the written standard.

The 2-minute summary

Sun protection involves more than one kind of biology.

1

Sunscreen reduces the damage, but not all of it.

SPF measures sunburn, not all the chemical activity sunlight causes. In lab studies some free-radical activity still happened under sunscreen, partly from visible light.

2

Some antioxidants can reach the living layers.

In donated human skin, green tea's main antioxidant (EGCG) was measured below the outer layer. These were experimental formulas, not Mama Sol products.

3

Zinc oxide stays on top, and that is the point.

A mineral filter works by sitting on the surface and blocking light. Several research methods find intact zinc oxide particles stay there.

4

Some ingredients help at the surface.

A 2% ectoin preparation improved skin hydration and surface measures in a controlled study of 104 women. Not every useful ingredient needs to travel deep.

5

Into the skin is not into the bloodstream.

In the lab studies reviewed, EGCG was found in skin layers but not in the fluid beneath the skin sample. Encouraging, but not proof for a living person.

6

Skin and body may be linked by signals.

In a small study of older adults, a barrier-repair moisturizer lowered some blood inflammation markers. Whether sunscreen does the same is untested.

Key ideas from the paper

Complementary-domain photoprotection

Mineral filters and skincare ingredients do different jobs in different places: blocking light at the surface, and supporting the skin below.

Four-compartment model

The paper maps skin into four compartments, from the surface down, to show where each ingredient has been measured.

Spatial mismatch hypothesis

An antioxidant may work only if enough of it reaches the layer where free radicals form, not just how strong it is in a test tube.

Reciprocal protection hypothesis

The mineral filter may help protect the antioxidants underneath it from breaking down in sunlight. Preliminary.

Every finding in the paper is graded by study type: A human in living people, B human skin samples, C lab-grown tissue or animal, D test tube, E regulatory review, F industry or patent literature.

Is mineral sunscreen safe during pregnancy?

By  Sheri Rowen, MD, FACS  · Updated  [date]
Short answerMineral sunscreens use zinc oxide or titanium dioxide. FDA has proposed that these two are generally recognized as safe and effective (GRASE), the strongest status a sunscreen active can have. Many doctors suggest mineral formulas during pregnancy, but always confirm your routine with your own OB or doctor.

Key facts

  • Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the two mineral sunscreen actives in the US.
  • FDA has proposed GRASE status for zinc oxide and titanium dioxide. In 2026 FDA also finalized bemotrizinol, a chemical filter, as GRASE.
  • Intact zinc oxide particles largely stay on the skin's surface, according to human imaging research.
  • Sun protection matters in pregnancy because hormone changes can make skin more prone to dark patches.

Why Mama Sol exists

"When I was pregnant with my first child, I started questioning everything I was putting on my skin," says Arielle Moody, CEO and Co-Founder. "I wanted a sunscreen with clean ingredients that I could feel good about using, but I didn't want to sacrifice how it looked or felt on my skin." That search became Mama Sol.
